# echo-experience
Toothed whale echolocation content for experiential exhibits

## Motivation

The goal of this repository is to provide underwater audio recordings of echolocation to support development of exhibits that educate listeners/visitors about how toothed whales use sound to navigate and hunt. The intent is for the content to be open access, clearly-licensed, and free for non-commercial use. Commercial users should seek permission via info@orcasound.net

The initial use of this repository is an echolocation experience at [The Whale in Andenes, Norway](https://thewhale.no).

### Gray whale call

These aren't echolocation clicks per say, but they are similar impulsive sounds. The type 1 call is sometimes said to resemble a conga drum because each pulse has tonality and the tone is varied in the sequence of pulses that make up a call. (You can hear the full gray whale call from which these sounds were clipped in the [Salish Sea gray whale catalog](https://www.orcasound.net/portfolio/gray-whale-call-catalog/).)

## Echo examples

NOTE: these are mostly synthetic echoes, created from volume backscattering models and/or edited manually in Audacity. For the manually-edited ones, the source sound may be an actual echo recorded in the ocean, but has still been modified.

For the echolocation experience at The Whale, we anticipate at least three visitors pointing clicking devices at a big interactive screen. Below are proposals for what the echoes from three prey targets should sound like -- from a salmon, a seal, and a squid. A simplification here is that the salmon has one echo from its swim bladder, the seal has a double echo (when its lungs are ensonified from the side), and a squid has a triple echo (when its pen, brain, and beak are lined up with the incoming click, i.e. facing towards or away from the whale).
